# *People in Honkai Impact 3rd, Be Their Heroes* — Volume Two Full Story and Continuity Guide

## Scope, sources, and the exact volume boundary

The work discussed here is **《人在崩坏三，当她们的英雄》**, written by **黄金森林** (Huangjin Senlin / “Golden Forest”), commonly rendered in English as ***People in Honkai Impact 3rd, Be Their Heroes***. This guide is intended as continuity-sensitive translation context rather than a loose synopsis. It records not only what happens, but also which version of history is currently real, which relationships survive Bai Ge’s time travel, what powers he actually retains, which apparently canonical events now have different causes, and which mysteries raised inside Volume Two are later confirmed by the already-established Volume Three continuity.

For chapter numbering and volume divisions, **Faloo is treated as authoritative** because it is the publication source. Chinese mirror text such as TRXS / Fan Novel Network is useful for recovering chapter prose that is difficult to expose through Faloo’s VIP pages, but mirror webpage numbers frequently do **not** equal Faloo chapter numbers. WTR-LAB is useful only as a secondary machine-translation/search aid. Established *Honkai Impact 3rd* English names are used where the correspondence is clear, while the fanfiction’s own altered lore takes priority over canon assumptions.

The volume boundary is unambiguous:

**Volume Two = chapters 53–99, inclusive.**

Faloo places **“第二卷 / Volume Two”** immediately before chapter 53 and **“第三卷 / Volume Three”** immediately after chapter 99. Volume Three begins with chapter 100, **“Joining St. Freya Academy, Living Together with Five Girls.”** Therefore Bai Ge’s formal St. Freya enrollment belongs to Volume Three, while the **successful return from the Sea of Quanta with Seele belongs to Volume Two** and must not be shifted across the boundary.

The corrected major structure of Volume Two is:

| Story phase | Chapters | Central development |

The most important correction compared with the earlier Volume-Two guide is therefore this:

> **Bai Ge does not merely prevent Bronya and Seele from undergoing X-10 and then watch fate restore it. He personally undergoes X-10 in their place, succeeds, acquires complete Quantum authority, and takes the fated X-10 burden away from them. Their later X-10 happens because they freely choose to search for him.**

That distinction changes the interpretation of the entire middle of the volume.

## The causal architecture of Volume Two

Volume Two is the first place where the novel stops treating historical change as simply **“Bai enters the past and saves somebody”** and begins explaining **how altered causality fights back, how Bai can permanently defeat that pressure, and how the people he saves acquire agency inside the new history**.

The central chain is:

> **Volume-One history rewrite → Current Era reorganizes around the rewrite → targeted traversal lets Bai attack a person's personal past → corrective fate repeatedly tries to restore X-10 → Bai discovers that merely blocking fate is temporary → he substitutes himself into the fixed point → the system declares the girls free → Bronya and Seele later choose a superficially similar X-10 path for a completely new reason → that free choice creates Seele's Sea-of-Quanta problem → Bai's successful substitution gives him the Quantum authority required to solve it → Mei's controlled awakening supplies the energy required to bring Seele home.**

That chain is the backbone of the volume.

### 1. The Current Era has already incorporated Volume One's rewritten history

Volume Two opens only an instant after Bai's return from the Second-Eruption traversal, but the world around him is **not** the old untouched 2014.

The system had already explained at the end of Volume One that historical correction is weak against changes Bai personally creates, but broader causal structures can still reorganize themselves. Several consequences are immediately visible:

- **Cecilia, Sirin and the other 2000 survivors remain alive somewhere in the world.**
- Theresa still creates St. Freya, but her relationship with Schicksal/Otto is much more hostile.
- K-423 still exists even though the original Sirin-as-Second-Herrscher route that should have produced her no longer happened.
- Mei's Gem of Conquest and K-423's Gem of Freedom physically recognize Bai as their historical bearer.

Thus Volume Two begins with a world already demonstrating a distinction that becomes crucial later:

> **History may preserve an important person/event while replacing the original causal route that produced it.**

This is not “the timeline undoing Bai.” It is the world **rebuilding continuity around him**.

### 2. K-423 is the first explicit example of causal reconstruction

Bai knows the original recipe for K-423: Kaslana material + Sirin/HoV material.

But Sirin never became the catastrophic Second Herrscher in Bai's rewritten history. **Bai did.**

The system tells him that highly important people such as K-423 can still be preserved by historical correction even if their original production route has been destroyed. Bai then infers the new recipe:

> **biological Kiana/Durandal's Kaslana material + Bai Ge's historical Herrscher-of-the-Void material.**

Volume Three later confirms that this is correct.

The rule established here should be carried forward:

**corrective adaptation can preserve an outcome while radically replacing the reason that outcome exists.**

### 3. Targeted past traversal is a different system mode from the original Past Simulator

The system unlocks a **new temporal mode** only after Bai has formed enough affinity with Current-Era characters.

The source defines its rules directly:

1. Bai may select a **person with whom he has established affinity**.
2. He travels specifically into **that person's past** rather than receiving a random major historical era.
3. The system may bind a strongly linked second target; choosing Bronya automatically binds **Seele**.
4. The mode has a **hard time limit**.
5. Bai **cannot select the temporary trait package** used in the original Second-Eruption Past Simulator.
6. The run still alters **real shared history**.
7. The run still produces **permanent completion rewards**.

The mode reduces randomness but increases personal responsibility. Bai knows whose tragedy he is entering.

At the moment it unlocks, Mei, Kiana and Bronya are eligible. Sirin and the other 2000 girls are **not selectable yet despite their enormous bond with Bai**, because Current-Era Bai has not physically met/reconnected with them in the present timeline. This proves that affinity alone is not the only requirement: the system also requires a valid **Current-Era relational connection/encounter** to use that person as a targeted anchor.

Bai selects Bronya partly because he wants more strength before solving Mei's Herrscher problem, but mainly because Bronya's childhood is the ugliest unresolved tragedy among the current trio.

### 4. The system supplies a local historical identity/body rather than sending teenage Bai unchanged

In Bronya/Seele's past, Bai does not arrive as the same visibly eighteen-year-old boy who is standing in Nagazora in 2014.

The system embeds him in the period as an **adult man nearing thirty** with a plausible local history: a former military officer whose unit was lost in a Honkai incident and who later founded an orphanage for children displaced by Honkai.

This identity is the foundation of the **House of the Hearth**.

The distinction matters later:

- historical Bronya remembers **adult Father Bai Ge**;
- Current-Era Bronya later sees eighteen-year-old Bai;
- both are genuinely the same system host;
- the age/body discrepancy is a consequence of the traversal's local identity, not evidence that Bronya remembers another man.

Volume Three later clarifies why other historical witnesses do not always instantly link past and present Bai: the system applies **temporal identity/cognitive protection**. That later rule should be imported backward as a clarification, not treated as something Volume Two characters themselves already understand.

### 5. The House of the Hearth is not only emotional history; it replaces the cause of Bronya and Seele's original route

Bai does not merely visit Bronya's childhood.

He creates a different social structure **before Cocolia can become the defining parent**.

The House gives Bronya, Seele, Rosaliya, Liliya, Sin Mal and the other girls:

- food and shelter without obedience being the price;
- an adult who protects them because they are children rather than useful assets;
- sisters/family;
- games, ordinary discipline, travel and domestic routines;
- a future that does not require treating sacrifice as love.

The critical historical replacement is:

> **Cocolia is still alive, but she is no longer the emotional center of Bronya and Seele's childhood. Father Bai Ge is.**

This changes the girls' later choices even when familiar plot-shapes return.

### 6. Corrective fate is stronger than ordinary historical adaptation

By chapter 82, Bai believes he has solved the problem by removing every obvious cause of X-10.

He has displaced Cocolia's parental position, protected Seele's natural stigmata, destroyed or frightened off hostile groups, and made the girls safe.

Then improbable disasters begin occurring anyway.

The system finally names the opponent:

> **`命运` — fate.**

Its explanation is unusually explicit. X-10 is not merely “an event likely to happen.” It is treated as one of Bronya and Seele's **most important fixed turning points**, so the world's corrective force tries to push them toward it regardless of which ordinary causal route Bai removes.

The system compares the situation to:

- **Mayuri's apparently unavoidable death in *Steins;Gate***;
- a target locked by the causal weapon Gungnir in *Dragon Raja*.

The point of those analogies is not canon lore. It is the system's definition:

> **some outcomes are causally locked strongly enough that ordinary avoidance only causes the world to find another route.**

Bai has already broken **37** corrective attempts, including the bizarre appearance of Parvati in an area he had made safe.

### 7. Bai can locally overpower fate, but the traversal timer makes that insufficient

The system then gives Bai the crucial qualification:

> **As long as Bai Ge remains physically beside Bronya and Seele, corrective fate cannot successfully harm them.**

He has already demonstrated this thirty-seven times. Whenever the world reaches for them, he breaks the route.

But only **five days remain** in the targeted traversal.

Therefore Bai's problem is not:

> “Can I protect them?”

He already can.

The real problem is:

> **“How do I create a result that remains protected after the system forcibly removes me from this era?”**

This deadline is why he cannot simply remain a perfect guardian indefinitely.

### 8. The solution comes from recognizing what Bai unconsciously did in Volume One

Bai initially asks why the Second-Eruption run did not trap him in the same kind of impossible correction.

The system tells him that it **did**.

He had already solved the same category of problem unconsciously. **Sirin and Cecilia's continued survival are evidence.**

Bai therefore reviews his first historical life and recognizes his recurring method:

> **when the world insists that somebody must pay a necessary price, Bai inserts himself into the payment position.**

That is the conceptual origin of the X-10 substitution.

He stops trying to make the fixed point disappear and instead changes **who bears it**.

### 9. X-10 succeeds for Bai; his disappearance is caused by the traversal timer

This distinction is essential.

Bai enters the X-10 chamber as the experiment subject **instead of Bronya or Seele**.

The experiment genuinely works.

His Honkai-energy manipulation progresses from atomic → subatomic → **quantum** scale. He gains real quantum power and demonstrates it by removing a test metal sphere from normal observable space into the quantum dimension.

Researchers explicitly celebrate:

> **X-10 succeeded.**

Then Bai begins fading.

The people in the room assume the successful subject is now suffering quantum collapse.

They are wrong.

The narration explicitly states that Bai **did not fail like Seele**. He has completely mastered the quantum power. His body is disappearing because the targeted traversal's countdown reaches zero.

The system confirms the causal victory before recall:

> because Bai underwent X-10, **Bronya and Seele's X-10 fate has been carried by him; corrective fate's influence over them disappears; from now on they are free.**

This is not metaphorical reassurance. It is the system's mechanical ruling.

### 10. Why Bronya and Seele later choose X-10 anyway

After Bai vanishes, the girls misinterpret what they saw.

From their perspective:

- Father Bai entered X-10;
- the experiment succeeded;
- his body then became immaterial/quantum-like and disappeared;
- Bai had told them he would be away for a very long time.

They do **not** know the system recalled him to 2014.

Therefore they form a plausible but wrong hypothesis:

> **Father Bai may have gone into the quantum dimension. X-10 may be a route to him.**

Bronya refuses to spend a year simply waiting. Seele and Sin Mal share the same impulse. The girls have internalized Bai's own ethic: love means acting rather than waiting passively for somebody else to suffer on your behalf.

That is why they return to the experiment.

The system later tells Bai with complete certainty that **fate did not force them**.

The resulting similarity to canon is therefore almost the opposite in meaning:

- old route → X-10 happens because the girls are trapped by circumstance/fate;
- rewritten route → Bai has already broken that fate; X-10 happens because the girls freely decide that reaching Father Bai is worth the danger.

Seele's attitude proves the point most clearly. When her own experiment begins failing, she is **hopeful**, because becoming quantumized seems to mean she may finally reach the same “side” as Bai.

### 11. The system-reward loop closes the X-10 causality

Bai's historical substitution does not only free the girls.

The system converts the achievement into a permanent Current-Era package.

The directly displayed rewards are:

- **Herrscher-of-Death stigmata**
- **Project Bunny 19C operation/control authority**
- **Initial Time authority**
- **Complete Quantum authority**
- **one villa/private island**
- **Infinite Evolution Body**
- **Heavenly Voice**
- plus additional minor/secondary rewards that the interface does not individually print before Bai stops checking.

The fact that the interface trails off with an ellipsis is a **source-level non-enumeration**. Future work should not waste effort trying to reconstruct an imaginary missing complete list from Volume Two unless a later chapter names a specific reward. The settled rule is:

> **the seven named rewards above are explicit; the system also gave additional secondary rewards that the source does not exhaustively enumerate in the immediate settlement.**

Among them, Complete Quantum, Initial Time, Infinite Evolution Body and the Death stigmata are strategically important.

The reward structure produces an elegant loop:

> **Bai takes X-10 to save Bronya/Seele → system permanently rewards him with the quantum power learned through that sacrifice → the girls later voluntarily enter X-10 looking for him → Seele becomes trapped in the Sea → Bai now possesses exactly the permanent ability required to go retrieve her.**

### 12. Historical rewrites update other people's memories; Bai's own subjective continuity does not reset

When Bai returns to 2014, less than one external second has passed.

Bai remembers the entire adult House-of-the-Hearth life he just lived.

The Current Era has also been rewritten. Present Bronya now possesses the childhood history Bai created and recognizes him as Father Bai.

That means two kinds of continuity coexist:

- **Bai remembers both the pre-traversal 2014 and the rewritten historical run.**
- **Bronya's Current-Era autobiographical history has been rewritten to include Father Bai and her later voluntary X-10 decision.**

The story does not present Bronya as carrying two equal conscious timelines and comparing them scene-by-scene. The reader and Bai know there **was** a pre-rewrite version; the corrected Current-Era Bronya lives from the rewritten history.

Later Volume Three's cognitive-protection rule explains the otherwise strange age/appearance issue: historical memory can persist while the system suppresses automatic identification across Bai's different traversal identities. Bronya's bond is strong enough that she recognizes him directly.

### 13. Bai's return has a real mental cost

The temporal runs consume essentially no external time but are not psychologically free.

After the Bronya/Seele traversal Bai returns profoundly exhausted. The source says **each time-travel return consumes a large amount of mental energy**; he feels that if he closes his eyes for more than a few seconds he may fall asleep standing.

He is also briefly confused about what “home” means because his first instinct is now the House of the Hearth, followed by memories of his original-world home, Mei's apartment and the system-provided residence.

This matters for characterization:

> **Bai's historical relationships are not disposable route memories. Each run adds another real home and another subjective life to the same person.**

### 14. Mei's controlled awakening is both preventive medicine and rescue logistics

After learning Seele is trapped, Bai already possesses the *method* required to enter the Sea.

He still lacks enough **available energy** to guarantee bringing another person out.

His body can tolerate more than roughly 4,500 HW by late Volume Two, but the five old Herrscher authorities are fragmentary and are not backed by five normal full cores that continuously provide huge reserves.

Opening a stable route from the Sea back to the proper world while carrying Seele requires **Herrscher-class energy**.

Meanwhile Mei still carries an unfinished Herrscher problem.

If Bai leaves that state unresolved, Cocolia or another enemy can trigger Mei later under hostile circumstances.

The two problems therefore solve each other:

1. deliberately complete Mei's awakening;
2. do it in empty countryside rather than Nagazora;
3. keep Mei emotionally stable and consenting;
4. let Thunder Queen finish becoming a human-aligned Herrscher;
5. use the Herrscher-level energy made available by that completed state as the reserve for the Sea rescue.

The controlled Third Eruption is therefore not a detached action arc. It is **Mei's liberation + prevention of future enemy control + the energy infrastructure for Seele's rescue**.

### 15. The Third Eruption proves that allegiance and classification are now separate

Completing Mei's awakening produces a real eruption-scale environmental response and approximately **110,000 Honkai beasts**.

But they do not obey her.

Thunder Queen had already rejected Honkai's will in Volume One, so the Honkai side treats her as a rebel rather than its rightful sovereign.

Theresa's response completes the institutional rewrite:

> **Do not attack Mei merely because she is a Herrscher; determine whether she retains human will and whether she is actually hostile.**

This policy exists because Theresa and many veteran Valkyries remember the rewritten Second Eruption, where Otto's “enemy Herrscher” was the one who died protecting them.

Thus Bai's old historical sacrifice becomes the reason Mei is given a fair chance in 2014.

### 16. The Sea-of-Quanta mission has a complete technical chain

Bai enters deliberately by using **Complete Quantum authority** to quantumize himself.

This is not Seele's uncontrolled experiment failure.

The Sea rescue then proceeds through four separate problems:

1. **entry** — solved by Complete Quantum authority, assisted conceptually by his Void/spatial ability;
2. **locating/reaching Seele** — solved through the quantum route and his knowledge of the setting;
3. **return energy** — supplied by the Herrscher-class reserve available after Mei's completed awakening;
4. **Kevin blocking the exit** — solved tactically rather than by defeating him outright.

Bai stacks:

- Wind acceleration;
- Thunder/electromagnetic acceleration;
- **Initial Time authority** to accelerate his own personal time.

Even that is not quite enough.

His final tool is information:

> **“Elysia isn't dead.”**

Bai knows Kevin's emotional history. The statement shocks him for the fraction of a second needed to close the route.

The sentence is neither a random lie nor proof that a physical 2014 Elysia is already waiting somewhere. It is a claim grounded in Bai's unique causal position:

> **if Elysia's death is a historical event, Bai's system can potentially go back and change it.**

Volume Four later makes that promise literal.

### 17. Volume Two's system rules should be carried forward as a single model

By chapter 99, the system has demonstrated all of the following:

| Rule | Established meaning |
|---|---|
| **External-time suspension** | A traversal can contain months of Bai's subjective life while essentially no time passes in the Current Era. |
| **Local historical identity/body** | The system can embed Bai into a target era with an age/body/background appropriate to that history. |
| **Targeted traversal** | Requires an affinity-linked Current-Era target, may bind another closely linked person, has a hard time limit, and does not provide the original freely chosen trait package. |
| **Real-history propagation** | Changes made in the traversal rewrite the shared Current Era and other people's autobiographical memories. |
| **Corrective adaptation** | Important people/events may be preserved through a new causal route after their original cause is destroyed. |
| **Corrective fate / fixed point** | Some major turning points exert stronger causal pressure and repeatedly regenerate routes toward themselves. |
| **Local resistance to fate** | Bai's physical presence can repeatedly break corrective attempts. |
| **Permanent substitution** | Bai can defeat a fixed burden by taking the required event/cost onto himself; X-10 proves this explicitly. |
| **Historical reward conversion** | Achievements/powers/body traits/material rewards from a run can become permanent Current-Era rewards. |
| **Mental cost** | External time barely moves, but Bai accumulates the subjective years and return exhaustion. |
| **Temporal identity protection — later clarified in Volume Three** | Historical memories persist, while automatic recognition between present Bai and traversal-era Bai may be cognitively suppressed; sufficiently strong bonds/evidence can overcome it. |

This complete model is more useful to future volumes than treating each system scene independently.

## Return from the rewritten Second Eruption: Mei, Kiana, and the changed K-423 problem

### Bai Ge returns after living another life

Volume Two begins almost at the same instant that Volume One ends.

For **Raiden Mei** and **Kiana**, Bai Ge has only been absent for an almost imperceptible period while staying at Mei’s home. For Bai Ge, however, the subjective gap is enormous. He has just spent months inside the rewritten Second Eruption, endured Babylon’s experiments, became the human-minded Second Herrscher, raised Sirin, Avrora, Agata and Galina as family, created Bella from Benares, fought Schicksal and Anti-Entropy, nearly died protecting enemy Valkyries from Otto’s fission weapons, saved Alexandra and unborn Bronya, rescued biological Kiana, and finally disappeared after Battle Continuation expired.

Consequently, chapter 53 presents a Bai Ge who is emotionally much hungrier for ordinary companionship than the girls remember. Mei’s food, Kiana’s presence and the domestic atmosphere of the apartment feel to him like a return home after another entire life.

This subjective-time mismatch becomes a recurring property of the system. Bai Ge can accumulate months or years of relationships in a past traversal while almost no corresponding time passes for the companions waiting in the Current Era.

### The Gem of Conquest and Gem of Freedom recognize him

Chapter 53 resolves a terminology point that the earlier guide left unnecessarily vague.

The two gems reacting to Bai Ge are:

- **Mei’s Gem of Conquest (征服宝石)**
- **Kiana/K-423’s Gem of Freedom (自由宝石)**

Both react because **Bai Ge was their historical bearer during the rewritten Second Eruption**. Fifteen years earlier, the past-simulation Bai Ge possessed those Herrscher powers/cores. In the altered Current Era, the gems now reside in Mei and K-423, yet they instinctively recognize the person who once carried them.

This reaction is not merely romantic atmosphere. It is physical evidence that Bai Ge’s simulated past has become **real history** for the Current Era.

### K-423 still exists even though Sirin never followed canon

The existence of present Kiana creates an immediate causal paradox.

Bai Ge knows that in the game’s original history, **K-423** was created by Otto through experiments combining Kaslana genetic material with Sirin/Herrscher-of-the-Void material. But this fanfiction’s history no longer contains that Sirin route. In 2000, **Bai Ge**, not Sirin, became the historical Herrscher of the Void and the Second-Eruption Herrscher whose biological/core material fell into Otto’s hands.

The system explains the broader rule: certain highly important people and structural events exert enough causal weight that the world may reconstruct a new route to preserve their existence even after Bai Ge destroys the original cause.

Bai Ge then reaches the correct conclusion in chapter 54: **the revised K-423 must have been produced using his own historical Herrscher/genetic material together with biological Kiana/Durandal’s Kaslana material.** The chapter plays this as the joke that Kiana should “call Dad,” but the causal reasoning is real.

Volume Three later confirms that this inference was correct. Therefore a translator should not preserve the old summary’s wording that Bai Ge’s involvement is merely an unresolved possibility. The precise continuity is:

**K-423 is a laboratory-created composite whose rewritten Herrscher-of-the-Void component comes from Bai Ge’s 2000 Herrscher self, while her Kaslana side comes through biological Kiana/Durandal material.**

This does **not** make Bai Ge her ordinary biological father, and Bai Ge himself never treats “K-423” as grounds to deny her identity as Kiana Kaslana. It explains the altered causal route by which K-423 still exists.

### Mei’s feelings continue beyond the old “fake relationship”

The “Dad” joke spills into chapter 55 when Mei hears Kiana using the term and briefly wonders what she herself should call Bai Ge. The comedy rests on an already-established emotional fact: the fake-dating arrangement from Volume One stopped being fake from Mei’s side long ago.

Mei trusts Bai Ge, is openly attached to him, and increasingly treats his continued presence as part of the life she wants to keep. Volume Two does not yet resolve every future romantic arrangement, but it makes clear that Bai Ge is no longer simply a classmate whom Mei pretends to date for protection.

## Bronya arrives in 2014: enemy operative, gaming rival, and healed girl

### Cocolia sends Bronya toward Nagazora

Volume One ended with Cocolia ordering **Bronya Zaychik** toward Nagazora after her controlled Third-Eruption plan failed. Chapters 56–60 finally bring Bronya into direct contact with Bai Ge.

Bronya initially approaches as someone operating under Cocolia’s influence and carrying the **Project Bunny 19C / 重装小兔19C** combat system. Bai Ge and Bronya do fight, but the strength gap is enormous. Bai Ge is no longer the ordinary human boy who fought Titans through desperation in Volume One. His permanent rewards include an enhanced body and fragmentary Herrscher authorities.

During the confrontation he uses his Thunder-related power strongly enough to overload Project Bunny and incapacitates Bronya without treating her as someone he wants to kill.

### Bronya refuses to buy Seele with a city

The crucial part of the encounter is ideological rather than physical.

Bronya’s deepest motive remains **Seele**. Cocolia has linked the possibility of recovering Seele to plans involving the Third Herrscher and Nagazora. Bronya desperately wants Seele back, but once she understands the true cost she rejects Cocolia’s calculus.

Her decision is not “Seele no longer matters.” It is the opposite:

**Seele matters enormously, but Bronya refuses to purchase her rescue with millions of innocent lives.**

This distinction is central to her character. Bai Ge does not replace Seele as the person she cares about most. He validates the desire to save Seele while proving that love does not require accepting Cocolia’s mass-casualty strategy.

### Alexandra’s survival

Bai Ge asks Bronya about her biological mother, **Alexandra Pavlovna Zaychik**.

The question is deeply personal because Bai Ge met the pregnant Alexandra during his Second-Eruption traversal. Otto later redirected a fission weapon toward her town, and Bai Ge destroyed himself intercepting it, thereby saving Alexandra and unborn Bronya.

Present Bronya explains that she was kidnapped very young, became a child soldier, and knows little about her biological mother before eventually entering Cocolia’s orbit. Bai Ge concludes from this altered history that Alexandra probably survived rather than dying along the route he remembers from canon.

Later continuity confirms that this inference is correct: **Alexandra remains alive in the rewritten world.** Therefore her Volume-Two status should not be left as a permanent uncertainty. What Volume Two establishes directly is Bai Ge’s evidence-based conclusion; later chapters confirm the survival.

At the same time, Bronya’s traumatic childhood still occurred. This gives Bai Ge his first clear lesson that changing one cause does not automatically rewrite every later event. Alexandra can survive and Bronya can still be kidnapped and become the Ural child soldier through another route.

### Bai Ge and Bronya settle things through games

Bronya notices that Bai Ge knows far too much about her family and history. Rather than interrogating one another violently, they turn the confrontation into a gaming competition.

Their first important game is ***Kallen Fantasy IV***. Bronya expects her elite gaming ability to guarantee victory. Bai Ge, however, possesses the permanent **Super Gaming Talent** reward earned from the Volume-One simulator and surprises her.

They ultimately play **ten games**, each selecting five titles.

**Final score: Bai Ge 6 – Bronya 4.**

What began as a hostile mission ends with the two genuinely enjoying one another’s company. This is one of Bai Ge’s recurring patterns: once he can get an opponent out of the factional role assigned to them and interact with the person underneath, the relationship can change extremely quickly.

### Bai Ge repairs Bronya while she is distracted

The gaming session also gives Bai Ge time to perform one of Volume Two’s most important medical interventions.

Using his fragmentary **Death authority**, he reconstructs the damage left by Bronya’s X-10 history at the cellular level. He repairs:

- the neurological damage affecting her emotional expression,
- her damaged legs,
- the physical systems that had forced her to rely on mechanical support,
- and Project Bunny 19C after the earlier overload.

Bronya does not immediately notice because she is absorbed in the games. After Bai Ge removes the leg-support equipment, she discovers she can **smile**, feel normally, stand and walk again.

The healing is complete rather than merely cosmetic. Her first steps are clumsy because she has not walked normally for years, but the underlying physical damage is gone.

This scene establishes a new continuity rule for the rest of the fanfiction:

> Injuries considered permanent by normal Schicksal or Anti-Entropy medicine are no longer automatically permanent if Bai Ge’s Life/Death-related authority can reconstruct the relevant tissue.

### Otto’s altered lesson from the Second Eruption

The comic *Kallen Fantasy* leaderboard plot simultaneously shows that Otto is still watching the Nagazora group. Bai Ge deliberately uses provocative usernames to insult Otto’s claim to be Kallen’s greatest admirer, and Otto takes the competition personally enough that he insists on winning honestly rather than simply abusing administrative control.

More importantly, Otto’s strategy toward K-423 has changed because of the rewritten Second Eruption and Mei’s Volume-One awakening. He now accepts that a Herrscher consciousness can be overpowered or redirected by sufficiently strong human bonds.

His plan is therefore to let K-423 accumulate friendships and emotional attachments at **St. Freya Academy** before her Herrscher personality fully awakens. Bai Ge’s actions have inadvertently taught Otto that affection is not irrelevant to Herrschers—it can be a decisive mechanism.

St. Freya itself is also already different from canon. Theresa’s relationship with Otto is badly damaged by the rewritten 2000 events, and many experienced Valkyries who survived Otto’s missile betrayal because Bai Ge protected them have either left Schicksal or moved into Theresa’s orbit. St. Freya is consequently much stronger and more institutionally independent than its original counterpart.

## The system unlocks targeted past traversal

### A new mode based on affinity

At the end of chapter 62, Bai Ge’s system unlocks a new temporal function.

The system defines it more precisely than a generic “choose a past” ability:

- Bai can select a **character with whom he has already formed system-recognized affinity**;
- he travels directly into that person's past;
- the system may bind a second person whose history is causally inseparable from the selected target;
- the traversal has a **hard time limit**;
- Bai **cannot choose the temporary trait package** available in the original Second-Eruption mode;
- the historical intervention still rewrites real history;
- and successful completion still gives permanent rewards.

The system explicitly lists Mei, Kiana and the newly encountered Bronya as available choices.

Sirin and the surviving 2000 girls are a useful edge case. They already have enormous emotional bonds with Bai from Volume One, but Current-Era Bai has not yet physically encountered them in the present timeline, so they are **not selectable through this mode yet**. This shows that the mode requires more than abstract historical affection: there must be a valid Current-Era target connection recognized by the system.

Bai chooses Bronya for two linked reasons.

Strategically, he wants to strengthen himself before solving Mei’s incomplete Herrscher awakening.

Emotionally, Bronya's childhood is the one he most urgently wants to rewrite: kidnapping, child-soldier life, Cocolia, X-10, disability and Seele's loss.

When he confirms Bronya, the system warns that her situation is special and automatically binds **Seele Vollerei** as a second historical target.

Thus the run is not “Bronya's past with Seele appearing incidentally.” It is mechanically a **joint Bronya–Seele targeted traversal**.

### Why Bai Ge chooses Bronya

The choice is partly strategic—Bai Ge wants to become stronger before completing Mei’s Herrscher problem—but the emotional reason is stronger.

Among the original trio, Bronya’s childhood is the one he regards as especially brutal: kidnapping, child-soldier conditioning, Cocolia’s orphanage, X-10, permanent neurological damage and the loss of Seele.

Bai Ge has already healed the present consequences.

Now he wants to attack the **cause**.

## The House of the Hearth: Bai Ge becomes Bronya and Seele’s father

### Adult Bai Ge and the orphanage

The targeted traversal does not insert Bai Ge into the past as his eighteen-year-old Current-Era body.

The system gives him both a **local historical identity and an adult body nearing thirty years old**. In the fabricated background he was once a military officer whose unit was lost during a Honkai incident. Disillusioned with the surrounding institutions and unwilling to abandon children orphaned by Honkai, he uses his life afterward to establish an orphanage.

This is a system insertion mechanism, not ordinary aging of the Current-Era body. Bai will return to 2014 as his teenage self when the traversal ends, while people inside the rewritten history remember the adult Father Bai Ge who genuinely lived beside them.

That orphanage is **壁炉之家 — the House of the Hearth**.

Use **House of the Hearth** consistently in English. “Hearth House” is a possible literal rendering, but switching between both names creates unnecessary continuity noise for an LLM translator.

The central hearth is not merely decoration. Warmth, fire, shared meals and an actual home in the Siberian cold become the emotional symbol of Bai Ge’s role.

The children call him **Father / Father Bai Ge**.

The household includes more than ten girls, with familiar members including:

- **Seele Vollerei**
- **Rosaliya Olenyeva**
- **Liliya Olenyeva**
- **Sin Mal**
- and later **Bronya Zaychik**

Bai Ge has lived in this local identity for roughly two months by the time the arc settles into its domestic rhythm.

As in Volume One, the system may fabricate a plausible starting history, but the relationships built afterward are emotionally real. Bai Ge stops treating “Father Bai Ge” as a disposable cover identity. He genuinely becomes the adult responsible for these children.

### Seele becomes one of Bai Ge’s daughters

Seele is already part of the House when the arc begins.

Her early chapters emphasize ordinary dependency rather than cosmic tragedy: she goes to Bai Ge when she cannot sleep, worries about him, wants to help with the household, and gradually treats him as the safest person in her world.

This matters because every later “Seele rescue” scene is now grounded in a real shared childhood. Bai Ge is not saving an acquaintance because he remembers a game plot. He is rescuing a daughter of the House of the Hearth who remembers him as Father Bai Ge.

### Raven’s island dream begins here

**Natasha Cioara / Raven** enters the altered past during this arc.

Her famous material dream—earning enough money for a villa and private island—is already present. The chapter title explicitly jokes that a person lives for a villa and island.

This motif survives far beyond Volume Two. When later volumes repeatedly destroy or threaten Raven’s property, the joke is paying off a personal goal already established in this period.

### The Ural Silver Wolf enters the House

The person Bai Ge is waiting for arrives in her **Ural Silver Wolf** phase.

This is child Bronya after kidnapping and child-soldier training, before the version of Cocolia’s orphanage history Bai Ge remembers can become emotionally central to her.

Bai Ge welcomes her into the House of the Hearth.

For perhaps the first time in Bronya’s remembered life, belonging is not conditional on usefulness, combat performance or obedience. She receives food, shelter, sisters, ordinary discipline, games and affection because she is part of the family.

The emotional replacement is enormous:

**Bai Ge—not Cocolia—becomes the defining parental rescuer of Bronya’s childhood.**

Chapter 69’s “Bronya’s tears of happiness” marks how completely this changes her route.

### Bronya wants to know Father Bai Ge

Bronya is naturally suspicious of inconsistencies. Bai Ge is far too capable, knowledgeable and financially committed to fit neatly inside his fabricated local history.

But her curiosity does not undermine the bond. The more she studies him, the more attached she becomes.

The arc deliberately gives Bronya and Seele experiences they were denied in the tragedy Bai Ge remembers: family meals, games, shared time, a seaside trip and the expectation that tomorrow will also contain the same people.

During the seaside sequence, Seele gets into danger in the water and Bai Ge rescues her. The event is played partly for romantic-comedy embarrassment in the source, but the continuity point is simpler: Seele’s dependence on and affection for Father Bai Ge deepens, while Bronya’s attachment to both Seele and Bai Ge becomes increasingly intense.

### Dark Seele becomes family, not an enemy personality

Seele’s second consciousness, **黑希儿 / Dark Seele**, becomes explicit during the arc.

The chapter titles make her decision clear:

**Dark Seele will protect Seele and Father Bai Ge.**

This is the correct continuity to preserve from this point onward. Dark Seele is not introduced as a demonic invader whom everyone needs to purge. She is abrasive, defensive and much more aggressive than White Seele, but her core motive is protective.

Bai Ge accepts her quickly.

Bronya’s reaction is also decisive: rather than rejecting the second consciousness, she treats the existence of two Seeles as **“double the happiness.”**

By the end of the House arc, both Seele personalities belong to the same family structure.

### Child-era filial language and later romance must not be flattened together

Chapters 79–84 increasingly use possessive and romantic-comedy language around Bronya and Seele’s attachment to Bai Ge, culminating in the chapter-84 title **“Ich liebe dich, Bronya, Across Fate.”**

For translation, two things must be preserved simultaneously:

1. During this childhood history, Bai Ge is genuinely **Father Bai Ge**, the adult who raised the girls.
2. The fanfiction later deliberately develops adult Bronya’s relationship with Bai Ge in a romantic direction after the Current-Era age/body context changes.

Do not rewrite the child-era relationship as secretly romantic from the beginning, but do not erase the emotional intensity that later becomes relevant either. The novel intentionally preserves both layers.

## Cocolia, Gray Serpent, and the world begins forcing X-10 back into history

### Cocolia has lost her old parental position

Because Bai Ge established the House of the Hearth, the familiar Cocolia Orphanage does not form around Bronya and Seele in the same emotional way.

Cocolia still exists. Bai Ge knows what the older Cocolia becomes and regards her as dangerous, but the past version is not identical to the 2014 antagonist. He intimidates her away from the House rather than simply murdering her and creating uncontrolled historical consequences.

This is important to the volume’s causal logic: replacing Cocolia in Bronya’s childhood does not erase Cocolia from the world.

### Gray Serpent recognizes the anomaly

**Gray Serpent** also visits during the House arc.

World Serpent therefore has historical contact with Bai Ge independent of its Volume-One surveillance of Nagazora. Bai Ge’s existence has now been inserted into a period and network where Gray Serpent can directly encounter him.

The meeting also links back to Raven. Gray Serpent is willing to offer material support, and Bai Ge redirects the logic toward the Natasha/Raven side of the household network. The villa/island joke continues through this contact.

### Bai Ge thinks X-10 has been eliminated

By chapter 82, Bai Ge believes he has removed the obvious path toward the original tragedy.

He has:

- replaced Cocolia as the central guardian,
- given Bronya and Seele a stable home,
- pushed Cocolia away,
- eliminated or frightened off groups targeting Seele’s natural stigmata,
- and prevented anyone from simply forcing the girls into X-10.

Then the world begins pushing back.

### Corrective fate escalates thirty-seven times

The pressure first appears statistically. Groups interested in Seele's stigmata suddenly become much more numerous.

Then increasingly improbable dangers appear. The most important example is **Parvati**, a high-level Honkai beast that manifests in an area Bai had already cleared and attacks Bronya.

Bai destroys it, but Bronya receives enough Honkai exposure to develop Honkai sickness.

He cures that too.

By combining his **Schariac holy blood** with Death-authority biological adjustment, Bai produces an anti-Honkai treatment comparable in effect to advanced anti-Honkai serum technology. Bronya's disease disappears the same day.

When Bai finally demands an explanation, the system identifies the opponent as **fate itself**.

It tells him that X-10 is a causally locked turning point in Bronya and Seele's lives. To explain the concept, the system compares them to **Mayuri's repeated unavoidable death in *Steins;Gate*** and to a target locked by the causal weapon Gungnir in *Dragon Raja*: if one route is blocked, the world attempts another.

The system then gives the number that proves how anomalous Bai is:

**he has already broken thirty-seven corrections.**

Normally even a tiny deviation should be pulled back by the next correction. Bai has personally shattered the “unavoidable” restoration **37 times in succession**.

The comparison to **Aponia** is therefore specific. Aponia could perceive fate and repeatedly discovered that trying to change what she saw produced worse results. Bai's advantage is not superior prophecy. It is that **history's correction has unusually little power over Bai himself**, so he can physically stand between the fixed point and its intended victims over and over.

The system explicitly states:

> **while Bai remains beside Bronya and Seele, corrective fate cannot successfully harm them.**

That sounds like victory until Bai looks at the traversal timer.

### The limitation: Bai Ge cannot remain in this traversal forever

The system gives him the terrible qualification:

**Bronya and Seele are safe from corrective fate while Bai Ge is physically present to intercept it.**

But the targeted traversal has a time limit.

If he simply disappears when the timer expires, the fixed X-10 fate can resume applying pressure after he is gone.

This is the real crisis of chapters 83–84.

The problem is no longer “how do I kill the next monster?”

It is:

**How does Bai Ge permanently remove X-10 from Bronya and Seele’s fated burden before he is forced back to 2014?**

## Bai Ge takes X-10 onto himself and wins against corrective fate

### The solution is substitution, not avoidance

Bai initially asks the system why his rewritten Second Eruption did not collapse under the same kind of fixed-point correction.

The answer is revealing: **it did confront him with the same category of problem, but Bai had already found the solution without consciously naming it.** The continued survival of people such as Sirin and Cecilia proves that some supposedly fixed tragedies were permanently broken.

Bai reviews what he actually did in that life and recognizes the pattern.

He did not merely stand nearby blocking consequences forever. He repeatedly **made himself the price** that history was trying to extract from someone else.

That insight gives him the X-10 solution.

If the world insists that the X-10 burden must be borne, Bai will not try to erase the existence of the burden.

He changes the bearer.

Instead of allowing Bronya or Seele to become the experiment subject, **Bai enters X-10 himself**.

The volume's key causal technique is therefore:

> **substitution beats correction because the fixed event is fulfilled, but its cost is reassigned to Bai Ge.**

This logic later becomes one of the novel's most important recurring problem-solving patterns, including the much larger Origin substitution in Volume Four.

### Bai Ge’s X-10 succeeds perfectly

The experiment does **not** fail for Bai Ge.

He successfully acquires **quantum power** and demonstrates it immediately by interfering with matter at the quantum level. A test metal sphere is removed from ordinary observable space and thrown into a quantum dimension.

The researchers celebrate because X-10 has produced a successful case.

Then Bai Ge’s body begins fading and losing ordinary material definition.

To the observers, this looks as though the apparently successful subject is now suffering the same kind of catastrophic quantum dissolution associated with an experiment failure.

That interpretation is wrong.

**Bai Ge’s X-10 itself has succeeded perfectly.**

His body is disappearing because the **targeted-past traversal timer has expired** and the system is removing him from that historical period.

The visual similarity between “quantumized experiment subject vanishes” and “time traveler is recalled to the Current Era” is what later causes Bronya and Seele to believe Bai Ge may have disappeared into the quantum dimension.

### Bai Ge shoulders Bronya and Seele’s fate

Before the traversal ends, Bai Ge asks the system whether his substitution worked.

The system confirms the result explicitly:

Because Bai Ge underwent X-10 for them, **the fated X-10 burden of Bronya Zaychik and Seele Vollerei has been taken onto Bai Ge himself. Corrective fate’s compulsory influence over that event disappears.**

The system’s conclusion is essentially:

**From now on, they are free.**

This changes how every later superficially canonical event must be interpreted.

Bai Ge did not merely delay their fate.

He **won this contest with corrective fate**.

### The second traversal’s permanent rewards

When Bai Ge returns to 2014, the system grants a much larger reward package than the earlier Volume-Two summary recorded.

The directly displayed rewards are:

- **Herrscher-of-Death stigmata (死之律者圣痕)**
- **Project Bunny 19C control/operation authority**
- **Initial Time authority (初级时间之权能)**
- **Complete Quantum authority (完全量子之权能)**
- **one villa/private island**
- **Infinite Evolution Body (无限进化之身)**
- **Heavenly Voice (天籁之声)**

The system display then trails off with an ellipsis. Therefore the source itself says there are **additional secondary rewards without immediately printing an exhaustive list**. Future work should not treat this as a missing research task. The seven items above are the explicit named settlement package; any later ability should be added to Volume Two's reward ledger only if a later chapter specifically identifies it as one of the omitted rewards.

The two most important for the rest of Volume Two are **Complete Quantum authority** and **Initial Time authority**.

Complete Quantum authority makes the Sea-of-Quanta rescue physically possible.

Initial Time authority is later used directly during the chapter-99 escape from Kevin, where Bai Ge accelerates his own time in addition to Wind and electromagnetic acceleration.

The **Infinite Evolution Body** is also important. Bai Ge’s already-enhanced body continues to evolve, and later in the volume he describes physical attributes vastly above a normal Herrscher baseline. This helps explain why the Current-Era Bai Ge can fight at a level far beyond what the “five fragmentary authorities” phrase alone would imply.

The Death-Herrscher stigmata also joins his existing fused-stigmata structure. Later prose describes his body as carrying a fusion of **Kaslana, Schariac and Seele/Death-related stigmata** rather than only the two inherited Volume-One bloodline packages.

The return also has a non-power cost. Bai notes that **every time-travel return heavily consumes his mental energy**. After this run he is so exhausted that he thinks closing his eyes for more than a few seconds could make him fall asleep standing. The external world has advanced by less than a second, but Bai has subjectively lived months as a nearly thirty-year-old father. This exhaustion and accumulated life experience should be treated as real continuity, not flavor text.

## After Bai Ge vanishes: Bronya and Seele choose X-10 anyway

### The family experiences Bai Ge’s disappearance as a death

For Bai Ge, the system has simply returned him to the Current Era.

For the House of the Hearth, their father has vanished in front of the world after an X-10 experiment and appears to have quantumized away.

The emotional damage is severe.

Bronya, Seele, Dark Seele, Sin Mal and the rest of the family do not possess Bai Ge’s outside knowledge that the system will place him safely back in 2014. From their perspective, he may be trapped somewhere beyond normal space—or dead.

Chapter 85’s title captures Bronya’s state bluntly: **“First day without Father Bai Ge, I want to die.”**

### Bai Ge changed their fate, but not their willingness to risk themselves for him

The girls have learned Bai Ge’s own values too well.

He spent the entire traversal refusing to let them remain passive victims while he knew a way to intervene. Once he disappears, they refuse to become passive victims of separation either.

They reason that X-10 and quantum-space research may offer a route to the place where Bai Ge vanished.

The crucial distinction is:

**Corrective fate is no longer forcing them into X-10.**

The system later confirms this with complete certainty.

They choose it themselves because they want to find Bai Ge.

### Bronya and Seele’s voluntary experiment recreates familiar surface events

Bronya and Seele eventually cooperate with Cocolia again for the purpose of carrying out X-10.

This produces results that superficially resemble canon:

- Bronya suffers neurological and leg damage.
- Seele becomes quantumized and disappears into the Sea of Quanta.
- Project Bunny 19C becomes the final connection/gift associated with Seele and Bronya.

But the causal structure is entirely different.

**Old/canon-like route:** Cocolia’s program and the girls’ tragic circumstances force them toward X-10.

**Rewritten route:** Bai Ge has already broken that compulsion. Bronya and Seele voluntarily approach the same danger because they believe Bai Ge disappeared into the quantum dimension and they want to reach him.

When present Bai later becomes angry that the outcome still resembles the old plot, the system answers directly that it is **100% certain corrective fate did not act on them after his substitution**. The girls themselves chose the path.

Seele even accepts the apparent experiment failure with hope because she thinks disappearing into the quantum side may finally put her in the same place as Father Bai Ge.

This is why the voluntary X-10 is not a partial failure of Bai's victory. **Their willingness to make the choice is evidence that they now possess the freedom he fought to give them.**

This is one of the most important examples in the novel of why **event-shape does not equal unchanged history**.

### Sin Mal stays with the House

Sin Mal does not simply vanish from the rewritten household after Bai Ge’s departure.

Present Bronya later explains that the other House-of-the-Hearth children remain safe and that **Sin Mal ultimately stays behind to protect the family**.

This is a major change to how she should be read. Whatever abrasive or unstable traits she retains, she has accepted the House as family strongly enough to protect it when Bai Ge is gone.

## Present Bronya’s history rewrites around Father Bai Ge

### Bai Ge returns to 2014 in less than a second

As with the first historical run, Bai Ge returns to the Current Era at essentially the same external moment from which he left.

Internally he has lived another long span as an adult father.

The system gives him the rewards listed above, but the immediate psychological effect is disorientation: he instinctively thinks of the House of the Hearth as “home” before remembering that his Current-Era home is with Mei and Kiana.

This is another reason Bai Ge’s emotional life becomes increasingly dense. He is not accumulating abstract “routes”; he is accumulating multiple real homes whose members remember him.

### Bronya now recognizes him

Before the traversal, present Bronya knew:

- kidnapping,
- child-soldier training,
- Cocolia,
- X-10,
- Seele’s loss,
- and none of the House-of-the-Hearth history with Bai Ge.

After Bai Ge changes the past, the Current Era rewrites.

Bronya now remembers:

- Father Bai Ge,
- the House of the Hearth,
- Seele and the other girls as that household family,
- the years of domestic life under Bai Ge,
- his X-10 disappearance,
- and her own later decision to search for him.

When she sees teenage Bai Ge in 2014, she recognizes him as the father she lost.

This is definitive proof that the targeted traversal changes **actual shared history**, not a private simulation or disposable side timeline.

### The apparent age contradiction comes from local-body insertion plus system identity protection

Bronya remembers Father Bai Ge as an adult nearing thirty in the past traversal, while Current-Era Bai is again an eighteen-year-old young man.

The adult body was the **local historical body supplied by the targeted-traversal system**. It was never evidence of a separate “Father Bai” soul.

Both are Bai Ge.

Volume Three later supplies the missing recognition rule: the system applies a **cognitive/identity protection** that suppresses easy linkage between present Bai and his traversal-era identities without deleting the historical memories themselves.

Bronya is an especially strong-bond case. Her rewritten life centers so heavily on Father Bai that she directly recognizes the present Bai rather than remaining blocked by the age/appearance discrepancy.

The complete rule to carry forward is therefore:

- Bronya's House-of-the-Hearth memories are real;
- adult Father Bai and teenage Current-Era Bai are the same system host;
- the traversal can supply different local bodies/ages;
- historical memories persist;
- the system may suppress ordinary cross-era identity recognition;
- sufficiently strong bonds/evidence can overcome that suppression.

This later-confirmed rule removes the need for future sessions to treat Bronya's recognition as an unexplained exception.

### Mei and Kiana meet “Bai Ge’s daughter”

When Bronya joins Bai Ge at Mei’s home, her paternal language creates immediate comedy. Mei and Kiana cannot understand how their same-age companion apparently acquired a teenage daughter.

Bai Ge, meanwhile, is exhausted after another long subjective life. The scene deliberately combines absurdity with domestic continuity: he has just left one family and returned to another.

Bronya and the Nagazora trio begin exchanging information about the different versions of Bai Ge they know.

The group structure now expands permanently:

**Bai Ge + Mei + Kiana + Bronya**, with Seele as the urgent missing family member who must be brought home.

## “The world must adapt to your existence”: the corrected meaning

Chapter 89’s title, **“The World Must Adapt to Your Existence, Host,”** is easy to oversimplify.

The strongest reading after checking the X-10 prose is not “canon endlessly defeats Bai Ge until the same outcomes happen anyway.”

The volume demonstrates three different causal states:

1. **Before Bai Ge solves the fixed point**, corrective fate actively generates increasingly improbable routes to force Bronya and Seele toward X-10.
2. **Bai Ge undergoes X-10 himself**, after which the system explicitly says he has taken their destined burden and corrective fate no longer controls that event for them.
3. **Bronya and Seele later choose a similar X-10 route voluntarily** because Bai Ge’s disappearance has become one of the most important facts in their lives.

Therefore the recognizable surface event returns for a fundamentally different reason.

The correct translation model is:

> **Important people and events can exert corrective pressure, but Bai Ge can break that pressure. Once his intervention becomes part of people’s real history, the world must continue from the choices and relationships he created rather than simply deleting him from the equation.**

This is why the present Bronya can still resemble familiar Bronya while simultaneously possessing an entire Father-Bai-Ge childhood that canon never contained.

Bai Ge has become one of the causal facts around which history now reorganizes itself.

## Preparing to rescue Seele: why Mei’s full Herrscher awakening matters twice

### Bai Ge can enter the Sea of Quanta by himself

The earlier summary correctly recognized that Bai Ge intends to rescue Seele, but it did not explain why he now has the means.

The answer is his **successful X-10**.

Because Bai Ge completely mastered quantum power, he can deliberately **quantumize his own body and leave ordinary reality**, entering the Sea of Quanta under his own control. He is not repeating Seele’s uncontrolled accident.

The story also clarifies why X-10 is relevant to Sea access in the first place. In this continuity, **World Serpent used Cocolia's X-10 route as an attempted artificial method of opening/accessing the Sea of Quanta and retrieving Kevin when ordinary routes such as Cosmic Juggernaut / the Eye of the Deep were unavailable**. Bai has effectively become the first perfect controllable success of a technology whose deeper purpose was always quantum access.

His fragmentary Void authority provides a second compatible route because the Second Divine Key’s quantum-space functionality demonstrates the connection between Void/space authority and access to the Sea.

Thus Bai Ge enters the mission with two mutually reinforcing capabilities:

- **Complete Quantum authority**
- **fragmentary Void authority**

He considers the combination reliable enough that, unlike almost anyone else in 2014, he can plan a deliberate round trip.

### Bringing someone back costs far more than entering alone

There is still one major limitation.

Entering the Sea of Quanta alone is relatively manageable for Bai Ge.

Opening a stable route back to the proper/current world **while carrying another person** requires a far larger Honkai-energy reserve. Bai Ge estimates that the operation needs at least **Herrscher-class energy**.

Conveniently, another problem is already waiting to be solved:

**Mei still needs to complete her Herrscher of Thunder awakening.**

This links the two arcs causally.

Bai Ge’s controlled Third Eruption is not only an attempt to stabilize Mei and stop Cocolia from forcing the awakening later.

It also creates the Herrscher-level energy reserve Bai Ge needs to bring Seele home.

## The controlled Third Eruption and the completed Herrscher of Thunder

### Bai Ge chooses the battlefield

Volume One stopped Cocolia’s planned Third Eruption by persuading Mei’s stigmata/Herrscher consciousness to reject Honkai’s destructive command.

That saved Nagazora, but Mei remained an incomplete Herrscher vessel carrying the Gem of Conquest.

Bai Ge recognizes the strategic problem. As long as Mei’s awakening remains unfinished, enemies can keep trying to trigger it under conditions they control.

His solution is to complete it **on Mei’s terms**.

He takes Mei, Kiana and Bronya away from Nagazora into an uninhabited area and uses a high-concentration Honkai-energy crystal associated with Cocolia’s preparations to supply the energy for a full awakening.

The difference from Cocolia’s plan is fundamental:

- Mei understands what is happening.
- Mei consents.
- Bai Ge, Kiana and Bronya are beside her.
- the site is chosen to minimize civilian casualties.
- the objective is stable mastery, not psychological collapse.

### Thunder Queen returns as an allied personality

As the transformation completes, the **Thunder Queen** personality re-emerges.

She remains clearly distinguishable from ordinary Mei:

- more arrogant,
- more commanding,
- more confrontational,
- and much more direct about her attachment to Bai Ge.

But her allegiance has not reverted to Honkai.

She remembers the Volume-One choice where Bai Ge refused to sacrifice Mei for “the world,” and she remains emotionally aligned with him.

This is why the correct translation is not “Mei is possessed by an evil Herrscher again.”

The situation is closer to **two Mei personalities sharing one body, both human-aligned, with the Thunder Queen possessing the active Herrscher temperament and authority.**

### The Honkai beasts treat Mei as a rebel

The awakening nevertheless produces a genuine Third-Eruption-scale Honkai reaction.

The surrounding area becomes saturated with Honkai energy and produces an enormous swarm—approximately **110,000 Honkai beasts** by Hyperion’s assessment.

However, the beasts do **not** obey Mei.

Because Thunder Queen previously rejected Honkai’s command, the Honkai side treats her as a rebellious Herrscher rather than a sovereign to follow.

Bai Ge therefore tells her to concentrate on stabilizing and completing the awakening while he, Kiana and Bronya hold the swarm back.

### Bai Ge, Kiana and Bronya fight together

This battlefield demonstrates the Current-Era group’s new strength.

**Kiana** fights aggressively in the usual Kaslana style.

**Bronya**, whose body and Project Bunny have already been repaired, deploys Project Bunny 19C at full function.

**Bai Ge** uses his fragmentary Herrscher package, evolved body, weapons and increasingly large energy tolerance to tear through upper-level Honkai beasts extremely quickly.

A Volume-One weapon also returns: **Plasma Kagehide / 等离子影秀**, which Bai Ge uses to dismember a Honkai Emperor-class opponent.

By this point Bai Ge’s combat effectiveness is stated to be comfortably above an ordinary S-rank Valkyrie.

### Hyperion arrives

Even the three of them cannot physically prevent every one of roughly 110,000 creatures from spreading toward populated regions.

Then **Hyperion** arrives.

For Bai Ge, this is more than seeing a battleship. As a former player, he recognizes the ship associated with the Captain role through which he once experienced the franchise.

The response force belongs to **St. Freya**.

Important people aboard include:

- **Theresa Apocalypse**
- **Murata Himeko**
- and the unusually strong St. Freya Valkyrie cadre created by the rewritten Second-Eruption history

Theresa knows Kiana is involved and is initially worried about her niece, only to discover that Kiana, Bronya and an unidentified white-haired boy are actively cutting through the Honkai swarm rather than helplessly awaiting rescue.

### Theresa refuses to repeat Otto’s mistake

Theresa gives one of the most important institutional orders in the early novel:

**Do not attack the Herrscher of Thunder merely because she is a Herrscher. First determine whether she retains human will and whether she is actually hostile.**

This policy is a direct consequence of Bai Ge’s rewritten Second Eruption.

Many Valkyries now know what happens when Schicksal labels a human-minded Herrscher an enemy and obeys Otto blindly: the “enemy” may be the very person who sacrifices himself to save them from Otto’s own weapons.

The altered St. Freya has learned from history.

Mei therefore receives a chance Bai Ge never received in 2000: she is judged by her actual behavior rather than exterminated on classification alone.

## Chapter 99: the rescue succeeds, and “Elysia isn’t dead” is a tactical continuity bomb

### The return route opens

Bai Ge gathers enough energy to interfere with reality at the quantum level and opens a blue route from the Sea of Quanta back to the **proper/current world (本征世界)**.

He takes Seele’s hand and begins the escape.

During this moment **Dark Seele briefly takes the front**, which Bai Ge notices from her changed demeanor and the more intimate interlocked grip. The personality switch is real but does not disrupt the escape.

### Kevin comes back at the worst possible moment

Kevin returns before the route can close.

Bai Ge cannot simply defeat him in a conventional sprint or overpower him with one authority. He therefore layers every acceleration method available:

- **Wind acceleration**
- **Thunder/electromagnetic acceleration**
- **Initial Time authority to accelerate his own time**

This is direct proof that the Time reward from the second traversal is already operational before Volume Two ends.

Even stacked together, Bai Ge still needs a tiny additional delay.

### “Elysia isn’t dead” freezes Kevin

Bai Ge realizes that he needs a sentence capable of making Kevin stop thinking like a combat machine for roughly a second.

He shouts:

**“Elysia isn’t dead!!!”**

Kevin freezes in shock.

That hesitation is enough.

Bai Ge and Seele enter the route, and it closes just before Kevin can reach it.

The statement’s exact role at the Volume-Two boundary must be translated carefully.

It is **not** proof that a physically living Elysia is already standing somewhere in 2014 waiting for Kevin.

It is also not an empty random lie.

From Bai Ge’s system-based perspective, **Elysia’s historical death is not an immutable final state because he can travel into and rewrite the Previous Era**. Volume Four later makes that future intervention explicit by sending Bai Ge into Elysia’s era.

Thus the clean translator-facing interpretation is:

> **“Elysia isn’t dead” is a future-changing promise/claim grounded in Bai Ge’s ability to rewrite her past, weaponized in chapter 99 to shock Kevin long enough to escape. Do not narrow it prematurely into ‘an Elysian Realm simulation is alive,’ and do not treat it as proof that present-2014 physical Elysia has already returned.**

### Bai Ge and Seele physically return to Mei’s home

The rescue is completed **inside Volume Two**.

Bai Ge and Seele emerge from the quantum route in **Mei’s home**, because Bai Ge sets his return coordinate to the place from which he entered.

White Seele returns to the front after the frantic escape.

Bai Ge tells her that whatever the detailed explanation, Kevin absolutely cannot be allowed into the Current Era yet.

This is the correct final plot state of chapter 99:

**Seele has been rescued. Bai Ge and Seele are back in the Current Era. Kevin remains behind in the Sea of Quanta.**

Any end-of-volume ledger that still lists Seele merely as “the objective of an ongoing rescue” is outdated.

Bronya is the clearest early demonstration.

This is still not yet the **full Current-Era Herrscher of Life and Death** state he obtains in Volume Three through the Gem of Serenity. Volume Two establishes the medical foundation; Volume Three upgrades it into full-scale authority.

### Complete Quantum authority

This is the largest new power of Volume Two.

It comes from Bai Ge’s **successful X-10 experiment** during the targeted-past traversal and is then granted/retained as a permanent system reward.

Capabilities demonstrated before the volume ends include:

Do not describe Bai Ge after chapter 84 as merely having “some quantum resistance.”

He possesses **Complete Quantum authority**.

The system reward list explicitly grants **Initial Time authority**.

Its first unambiguous major use is chapter 99, where Bai Ge accelerates his personal time while escaping Kevin.

This is separate from the system’s time-travel functions. One is a combat/physical authority over time; the other is the system mechanism that sends Bai Ge into historical periods.

### Infinite Evolution Body

The second traversal also leaves Bai Ge with **Infinite Evolution Body**.

His physical strength, durability, endurance and regeneration continue to develop beyond his earlier Herrscher-reconstructed baseline. Late-Volume-Two narration emphasizes that his raw body has become absurdly strong even without relying exclusively on authority attacks.

This body state helps explain why his effective combat level exceeds an ordinary S-rank Valkyrie even though his five old Current-Era Herrscher powers remain fragmentary.

The reward list includes a **Herrscher-of-Death stigmata**. Later prose folds this into Bai Ge’s existing fused-stigmata structure alongside Kaslana and Schariac inheritances.

For translation, do not confuse this stigmata with a full physical Death/Life core.

The clean category distinction is:

- **stigmata inheritance/reward** = body/ability structure,
- **fragmentary Death authority** = reduced Current-Era Herrscher ability from Volume One,
- **full Gem-of-Serenity Life/Death core/state** = later Volume Three acquisition.

### Energy capacity

By the late volume Bai Ge says he can tolerate roughly **more than 4,500 HW** of Honkai energy.

Yet because he lacks five ordinary full cores supplying his old fragments, giant operations can still demand external energy.

This is why the completed Herrscher-of-Thunder awakening is also used to support the Sea-of-Quanta rescue.

## Altered history and causal rules established by Volume Two

### K-423 is preserved through a new cause

K-423 still exists despite Sirin’s radically rewritten Second-Eruption history.

The world preserves the important person through a different creation route, and that route now uses **Bai Ge’s historical Herrscher material**.

This is an example of corrective adaptation, not proof that Bai Ge failed to change history.

### Alexandra survives while Bronya still becomes the Ural Silver Wolf

Bai Ge saves pregnant Alexandra in 2000.

Later continuity confirms she survives.

Yet Bronya is still kidnapped and becomes a child soldier through another route.

The person/event pattern can partially persist even after one underlying tragedy has been removed.

### X-10 is the clearest demonstration that “same event” can mean opposite causality

The world initially attempts to force X-10 back into Bronya and Seele’s path.

Bai Ge defeats that compulsion by taking the experiment himself.

The system explicitly says the girls are free.

They later recreate a superficially similar X-10 outcome **by their own choice**, because finding Bai Ge matters enough to them that they willingly accept the danger.

Therefore:

**Surface similarity to canon must never be used by a translator as evidence that Bai Ge’s rewrite failed.**

Always ask **why** the event happens in this continuity.

### The system’s past is real history, and memory propagation is asymmetric

Present Bronya acquiring years of memories with Father Bai proves that targeted traversal alters the **shared Current-Era world**.

The system is not generating a private dream and merely copying rewards into Bai.

People outside Bai remember the new history.

The important memory model is:

- Bai remembers the **pre-change Current Era** because he initiated the traversal from it;
- Bai also remembers the **new historical life** he personally lived;
- people whose past was rewritten grow into the Current Era with the **rewritten autobiographical history**;
- they are not normally presented as consciously carrying a second untouched-canon memory track;
- later system identity protection can suppress recognition of Bai across differently aged/historical bodies without erasing the actual historical memories.

This is why Bai can remember first meeting a Bronya who did not know him while present Bronya now remembers Father Bai as part of her real childhood.

### Bai Ge is becoming a causal anchor

By the end of the volume, history can no longer treat Bai Ge as a visitor whose deviations will simply be smoothed away.

His relationships are now causes:

- Bronya chooses X-10 because of him.
- Seele enters the Sea because she hopes to find him.
- K-423’s revised existence derives partly from his historical body.
- Mei completes her Herrscher awakening under his plan.
- Theresa’s Herrscher policy reflects what he did in 2000.
- Kevin’s next decisions are altered by what Bai Ge tells him about Elysia.

This is the proper meaning of the world having to **adapt to Bai Ge’s existence**.

## System functions established by the end of Volume Two

| System function / reward class | Established behavior by chapter 99 |
|---|---|
| **Historical Past Simulator** | Sends Bai Ge into a major earlier era with a local identity and temporary traits; history changes are real; bonds/achievements can become permanent rewards. |

| **Corrective adaptation** | Important people/events may reappear through a different causal route when Bai destroys the original cause; K-423 is the clearest early example. |
| **Corrective fate / fixed point** | Some life-defining events exert much stronger correction pressure. Ordinary prevention causes new routes to form; X-10 produces at least 37 corrections before Bai solves it. |
| **Bai's local resistance to correction** | While Bai remains physically beside the intended victims, fate's corrections can be broken repeatedly because historical correction has unusually weak leverage over him. |
| **Substitution of a fixed burden** | Bai can permanently defeat a fixed point by becoming the person who fulfills/pays the required event himself. His successful X-10 removes corrective compulsion from Bronya and Seele. |
| **Local historical body/identity** | Targeted traversal can insert Bai at a different apparent age with a fabricated local biography while remaining the same system host. |
| **Temporal identity protection — clarified later** | Rewritten memories persist, but the system can suppress easy recognition between present Bai and his traversal identities. Strong bonds/evidence can break through. |
| **Complete Quantum authority** | Granted after Bai Ge successfully takes X-10; lets him deliberately quantumize, enter the Sea and open a return route with enough energy. |
| **Initial Time authority** | Combat/physical time manipulation separate from historical travel; directly used to accelerate Bai Ge during chapter 99. |
| **Infinite Evolution Body** | Preserves and extends the second traversal’s extreme physical evolution. |
| **Causal memory rewrite** | Present people can acquire memories created by Bai Ge’s changed past, as Bronya does after the House-of-the-Hearth traversal. |

A translator should keep **system time travel** and **Time authority** conceptually separate.

## Settled source boundaries — what future work does **not** need to reopen Volume Two to answer

The following points are fully settled by Volume Two plus direct later confirmation and should not be treated as research questions again:

- **X-10 substitution worked.** Bai's experiment succeeded; his disappearance was traversal recall.
- **Corrective fate over Bronya/Seele's X-10 was broken permanently.**
- **Their later X-10 was voluntary.**
- **Seele is already rescued by chapter 99.**
- **Kevin remains in the Sea at the boundary.**
- **Complete Quantum and Initial Time are permanent powers, not temporary historical effects.**
- **Infinite Evolution Body and Death-Herrscher stigmata are permanent rewards.**
- **K-423's rewritten HoV material comes from historical Bai**, later confirmed explicitly in Volume Three.
- **Bronya's Father-Bai memories are real rewritten history.**
- **Adult Father Bai and teenage Current-Era Bai are the same person operating through different traversal/current bodies.**
- **The system can later be understood to protect cross-era identity recognition without deleting historical memories.**
- **Cocolia is still free/alive at the exact chapter-99 boundary; her permanent neutralization belongs to early Volume Three.**
- **Bai is not yet Hyperion's certified captain at chapter 99.**
- **“Elysia isn't dead” is not proof of an already-present 2014 Elysia; it is a claim grounded in Bai's ability to rewrite the Previous Era.**

There are only a few genuine source-level limits:

1. The reward interface after the Bronya/Seele traversal prints seven named rewards and then an ellipsis. The novel itself does not immediately enumerate every minor additional reward. Treat the seven explicit items as authoritative and add omitted rewards only when later prose names them.
2. Volume Two does not yet explain the full system **cognitive identity-protection** mechanism; Volume Three does. This guide imports that later clarification solely to close the age/recognition logic.
3. The complete long-term consequences of “Elysia isn't dead” belong to Volume Four, not Volume Two.

These are chronological/source boundaries, not missing continuity research.

## Final translator-facing synthesis

Volume One taught Bai Ge that changing tragedy sometimes means **putting himself in the place where another person was supposed to suffer**.

Volume Two expands that principle from sacrifice into causality.

Bai Ge first learns that the world can reroute history around his interventions: K-423 still exists even though Sirin’s past was rewritten; Bronya can still become the Ural Silver Wolf even though Alexandra survived. During the House-of-the-Hearth traversal he then confronts corrective fate directly. The world repeatedly attempts to force Bronya and Seele toward X-10, and Bai Ge responds not by endlessly blocking accidents but by **taking X-10 onto himself**.

He succeeds.

The system explicitly tells him that he has shouldered the girls’ fixed fate and that they are free.

The apparent irony is that Bronya and Seele later choose X-10 anyway—but that is precisely what proves Bai Ge won. They are no longer puppets of a fixed route. They enter the danger because **Father Bai Ge disappeared and they choose to search for him**. The same event-shape now expresses their agency and relationship with him rather than the world’s control over them.

That choice gives Bai Ge the power needed to close the loop. His successful X-10 becomes permanent **Complete Quantum authority**; his system also grants **Initial Time authority** and a new evolution/stigmata package. Back in 2014, he uses those abilities to plan a real rescue. Mei’s controlled Herrscher awakening is simultaneously the completion of her own liberation from Cocolia and the Herrscher-level energy source Bai Ge needs for the Sea of Quanta.

He then does what he promised.

He enters the Sea deliberately, finds Seele, meets Kevin, refuses to let Project STIGMA’s author simply walk back into 2014, opens a path home and escapes with Seele. His final weapon is information: **“Elysia isn’t dead.”** The sentence stops Kevin long enough for the route to close and points directly toward the Previous-Era intervention that later volumes will make possible.

By the end of chapter 99, Volume Two’s core thesis is no longer merely that Bai Ge can change history.

It is:

> **The people Bai Ge saves can now choose new futures because of the relationships he inserted into history—and the world increasingly has to build itself around those choices rather than erase them.**

That is the state from which Volume Three begins.
